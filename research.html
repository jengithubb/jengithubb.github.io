Longitudinal radiopathomic research.

I study quantitative MRI-derived features in glioblastoma through computational imaging, longitudinal feature engineering, and statistical analysis. This overview introduces the questions, methods, and research contributions behind my work.

First-author manuscript in preparation

An overview of the research approach; detailed findings are not yet publicly available.

01Biomedical question

How can pathology-informed imaging characterize tumor heterogeneity and temporal changes?

02Imaging inputs

Multiparametric MRI and existing machine-learning–generated cellularity and tumor probability maps.

03Longitudinal context

Pre-radiotherapy, post-radiotherapy, pre-progression, and progression imaging.

01 / RESEARCH CONTEXT

From images to quantitative evidence

Conventional MRI is an important tool for monitoring glioblastoma, but imaging appearance does not always uniquely represent underlying biological processes. My project examines how quantitative, pathology-informed maps can be analyzed alongside conventional lesion regions and clinical data to better characterize measurable tumor features over time.

The underlying radiopathomic mapping models were developed by external collaborators; my research centers on downstream image processing, feature-extraction design, longitudinal analysis, and statistical evaluation.

02 / COMPUTATIONAL METHODS

Two complementary feature-extraction strategies

A · ROI BASED

Region-level distribution features

Characterize imaging-derived distributions in contrast-enhancing and non-enhancing lesion regions, using summary statistics, upper-percentile measurements, heterogeneity descriptors, and lesion volume.

B · THRESHOLD BASED

High-value region quantification

Examine threshold-defined regions on cellularity and tumor probability maps, with visualization-guided quality control, morphological cleaning, connected-component filtering, and volumetric feature extraction.

03 / LONGITUDINAL DESIGN

Changes across clinical timepoints

Pre-RTPost-RTPre-progressionProgression

My analysis integrates feature measurements at each available timepoint with within-patient change metrics. This includes distributional and volumetric features, while addressing missing scans, repeat observations, alignment, and consistency of image-derived measurements.

04 / STATISTICAL APPROACH

Clinical and statistical evaluation

My analytical work includes Kaplan–Meier curves, Cox proportional hazards models, Spearman correlation, and linear regression, including multivariable adjustment for relevant clinical covariates, model diagnostics, and false discovery rate correction. These methods support careful examination of relationships between quantitative imaging characteristics and clinical endpoints.

05 / MY CONTRIBUTIONS

What I worked on

  • Designed ROI-based and threshold-based strategies for extracting longitudinal quantitative imaging features.
  • Applied and adapted existing MRI preprocessing workflows; addressed image alignment, segmentation quality, normalization, missing imaging, and repeated measurements.
  • Evaluated threshold options using map visualization and quality-control checks; quantified cleaned abnormal regions and tumor volume.
  • Conducted statistical and survival analyses and produced figures for the first-author manuscript.
  • Worked across image data, clinical variables, research interpretation, and scientific communication.

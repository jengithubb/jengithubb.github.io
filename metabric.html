Hormone Therapy & Relapse-Free Survival

An observational survival analysis of estrogen receptor–positive breast cancer in the METABRIC cohort, examining how covariate adjustment, missing-data handling and proportional-hazards diagnostics influence the interpretation of treatment-associated relapse-free survival.

Clinical question

Is receipt of hormone therapy associated with relapse-free survival among patients with estrogen receptor–positive (ER+) breast cancer? Because treatment was not randomized, the analysis considers differences in patient demographics, tumor characteristics and other treatments rather than treating an observed association as a causal effect.

Data & cohort construction

The supplied METABRIC clinical table contains 2,509 patients. Selecting ER-positive cases with complete hormone-therapy and outcome information yields 1,497 patients: 1,104 who received hormone therapy and 393 who did not. The final workflow retains five undefined HER2 measurements as missing values for imputation; the earlier exclusion workflow used 1,492 patients.

The endpoint is relapse-free survival, defined in the source data as locoregional or distant relapse, or disease-specific death. An independent input audit confirmed the revised cohort size.

Analytical approach

01 / Missing data

Examined missing clinical covariates and used multiple imputation by chained equations (five imputations) to retain analyzable records, pooling regression estimates with Rubin’s rules.

02 / Survival models

Compared Kaplan–Meier curves, unadjusted and covariate-adjusted Cox regressions, and a Cox specification stratified by surgery type and cohort.

03 / Diagnostics

Evaluated proportional-hazards assumptions with Schoenfeld residuals and explored stratification to address substantial violations.

04 / Exploratory checks

The archived R Markdown includes exploratory subgroup checks by chemotherapy exposure, HER2 status and menopausal status. The cleaned entry point focuses on the primary models; subgroup findings are not confirmatory evidence of treatment-effect differences.

How adjustment changed the estimate

The hazard ratios below are retained from Table 3 of the original course report. They have not been refitted in the cleaned repository; the data audit verifies cohort construction, not the historical model estimates.

The unadjusted hazard-ratio estimate was above 1, whereas estimates following clinical covariate adjustment were below 1. All three 95% confidence intervals included 1, so the analysis did not demonstrate a statistically significant association between hormone therapy and relapse-free survival.

Interpretation & limitations

Differences between treated and untreated patients underscore the risk of confounding by indication in retrospective clinical datasets. The shift after adjustment demonstrates why model specification matters, but does not prove hormone therapy caused a reduction in the risk of relapse or disease-specific death. The dataset also lacks detailed timing, dosage, duration and adherence information, and residual confounding may remain.

Research skills demonstrated

  • Clinical data cleaning, cohort definition and missingness assessment
  • Kaplan–Meier estimation and multivariable Cox regression
  • Multiple imputation and pooled statistical inference
  • Schoenfeld residual diagnostics, model stratification and sensitivity analysis
  • Careful interpretation of observational treatment–outcome associations

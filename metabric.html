CLINICAL BIOSTATISTICS / BIOSTAT 209 FINAL PROJECT

Hormone Therapy & Breast Cancer Recurrence

An observational survival analysis of estrogen receptor–positive breast cancer in the METABRIC cohort, examining how covariate adjustment, missing-data handling and proportional-hazards diagnostics influence the interpretation of treatment-associated recurrence.

Jennifer Zhu · BIOSTAT 209 · 2025

Clinical question

Is receipt of hormone therapy associated with recurrence-free survival among patients with estrogen receptor–positive (ER+) breast cancer? Because treatment was not randomized, the analysis considers differences in patient demographics, tumor characteristics and other treatments rather than treating an observed association as a causal effect.

Data & cohort construction

Using the publicly available METABRIC clinical dataset, the R analysis filters patients to ER+ status, removes undefined HER2 measurements, and excludes records missing the exposure or recurrence outcome. Re-running those deterministic filtering steps on the project’s supplied data yields 1,492 patients: 1,101 who received hormone therapy and 391 who did not.

Data audit: The original course report contains inconsistent cohort totals (1,477 and 1,497). The 1,492 figure above is verified from the supplied data and R cleaning logic. No patient-level data are embedded in this webpage.

Analytical approach

01 / Missing data

Examined missing clinical covariates and used multiple imputation by chained equations (five imputations) to retain analyzable records, pooling regression estimates with Rubin’s rules.

02 / Survival models

Compared Kaplan–Meier curves, unadjusted and covariate-adjusted Cox regressions, and a Cox specification stratified by surgery type and cohort.

03 / Diagnostics

Evaluated proportional-hazards assumptions with Schoenfeld residuals and explored stratification to address substantial violations.

04 / Exploratory checks

The R Markdown also includes sensitivity and subgroup analyses by chemotherapy exposure, HER2 status and inferred menopausal status. These are exploratory, not confirmatory evidence of treatment-effect differences.

How adjustment changed the estimate

The course report illustrates an important feature of observational research: the unadjusted hazard-ratio estimate was above 1, whereas adjusted estimates were below 1. None of the three reported 95% confidence intervals excluded 1, so the analysis did not establish a statistically significant association between hormone therapy and recurrence-free survival.

Interpretation & limitations

Differences between treated and untreated patients underscore the risk of confounding by indication in retrospective clinical datasets. The shift after adjustment demonstrates why model specification matters, but does not prove hormone therapy caused a reduction in recurrence. The dataset also lacks detailed timing, dosage, duration and adherence information, and residual confounding may remain.

Before making stronger claims, the analysis would benefit from fully reproducible pooled-model outputs, rechecking the post-stratification PH tests, and validating imputation methods appropriate for categorical variables.

Research skills demonstrated

  • Clinical data cleaning, cohort definition and missingness assessment
  • Kaplan–Meier estimation and multivariable Cox regression
  • Multiple imputation and pooled statistical inference
  • Schoenfeld residual diagnostics, model stratification and sensitivity analysis
  • Careful interpretation of observational treatment–outcome associations

BAYESIAN BIOSTATISTICS / BIOSTAT 226

Bayesian Modeling of Breast Cancer Treatment Response

Investigating how pretreatment MRI-derived tumor characteristics and molecular receptor status relate to pathologic complete response using interpretable Bayesian logistic regression.

Jennifer Zhu · Individual course project · March 2026

Rbrms / StanI-SPY2Breast MRIMCMCLOO-CV
384Complete-case observations
3Bayesian models compared
8,000Post-warmup draws per fitted model

Research question

Can baseline MRI tumor longest diameter and sphericity help characterize the probability of pathologic complete response (pCR) to neoadjuvant treatment? Do hormone receptor (HR) and HER2 status provide additional predictive information?

Using clinical and imaging features from the I-SPY2 imaging cohort, the project examined the relative contribution of imaging and receptor-status features while explicitly quantifying uncertainty in model estimates.

Data and study design

Clinical outcomes and MRI-derived measurements were joined using participant identifiers. The analysis focused on four pretreatment predictors: longest tumor diameter (LD), tumor sphericity, HR status and HER2 status. After selecting records with complete data for the outcome and predictors, 384 observations were included. The two continuous imaging predictors were standardized before modeling.

Imaging features

Baseline longest diameter (LD_T0) and tumor sphericity (SPHERICITY_T0), representing tumor size and shape.

Molecular markers

Hormone receptor status and HER2 status, included to evaluate their contribution beyond imaging measurements.

Bayesian modeling workflow

01 / Prior specification

Specified regularizing normal priors for standardized imaging predictors and receptor-status coefficients, reflecting plausible effect sizes on the log-odds scale.

02 / Posterior estimation

Fit Bernoulli logistic regression models with brms and four MCMC chains, using 4,000 iterations per chain and 2,000 warmup iterations.

03 / Diagnostics

Examined posterior predictive checks, trace plots, R-hat statistics and effective sample sizes to assess mixing and convergence.

04 / Model comparison

Compared imaging-only, additive imaging-plus-receptor, and HR × HER2 interaction specifications using leave-one-out cross-validation.

Posterior estimates

In the selected additive model, the molecular markers showed clearer associations with pCR than either baseline imaging measure. Odds ratios and 95% Bayesian credible intervals from the project analysis are shown below.

Model comparison

Three specifications tested how different feature groups contributed to expected out-of-sample predictive performance. The additive model offered a strong balance between predictive fit and parsimony.

Model 1 · MRI featuresΔELPD −17.9
Model 2 · MRI + HR + HER2ΔELPD 0.0
Model 3 · Plus HR × HER2ΔELPD −0.5

The bars are a visual guide to relative model ranking, not a probability scale. Model 3 performed similarly to Model 2, without a meaningful improvement from adding the interaction.

Model checking and sensitivity

For the preferred model, reported R-hat values were 1.00 and effective sample sizes were high, supporting adequate MCMC mixing in the recorded run. A sensitivity analysis replacing the specified priors with default priors yielded broadly similar coefficient directions and magnitudes.

Interpretation and limitations

In this cohort, HR and HER2 status contributed more predictive information about pCR than baseline tumor diameter or sphericity. The baseline imaging features were still included to test whether simple pretreatment size and shape measurements provided complementary information.

The analysis was limited to complete cases and a small set of imaging features, and the results should not be interpreted as a clinically validated prediction tool. LOO comparisons assess relative expected predictive performance for the fitted models; independent external validation would be required before clinical use.
